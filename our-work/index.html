The GuateStar Pathway

Two paths, six stages, from Club Extra to college

Every child keeps studying while learning to lead. This is how we walk with them, stage by stage.

  1. Home
  2. The Pathway

The Pathway is two parallel paths: school and leadership

Everyone starts at Club Extra. From there, the school path leads to a high school or INTECAP scholarship and then to college or a technical program. At the same time, many participants become volunteers, then facilitators, and in high school they receive business mentoring. You don’t have to finish one path to start the other.

Stage by stage

Who it’s for, what young people do and how they move ahead

Stage 1 · School path

Club Extra

Who it’s for
Children ages 6 to 12, from preschool through 6th grade, in eight rural communities of Totonicapán.
What they do
They meet every week in their own community to learn with computers and the CREA+ method (Connect, Solve, Experiment, Argue and Improve), created by GuateStar. They also have Chispa, an AI tutor that helps them through questions without giving away the answer.
How to move ahead
Children who stand out for their curiosity, whom we call “chispudos” (bright sparks), are invited from 4th grade to become assistants, helping a volunteer or facilitator. That is where the leadership path begins.

Stage 2 · Leadership path

Volunteer

Who it’s for
Middle school students who started as assistants at Club Extra.
What they do
Supports the facilitators, prepares materials and helps younger children during sessions.
How to move ahead
After one year as a volunteer, they become a facilitator.

11 volunteers4 of them are high school scholarship students

Stage 3 · Leadership path

Facilitator

Who it’s for
Young people with at least one year of service as volunteers.
What they do
Leads Club Extra sessions with the CREA+ method and is a close role model for the children in their community.
How to move ahead
Between ages 14 and 17 they can take part in business mentoring.

6 facilitators

See the program
Stage 4 · Leadership path

Business mentoring

Who it’s for
Young people ages 14 to 17, from 8th grade through high school. They are not the mentors: they are students who receive mentoring.
What they do
Over 16 weeks they work in teams on business challenges, guided by mentors, and present their solutions to a panel of judges. The program is called “Mentores.”
How to move ahead
It prepares young people to take the step to college or a technical program.

14 of 22 completed22 young people started in 2026 and 14 completed all 16 weeks

Stage 5 · School path

High school scholarship

Who it’s for
Young people 15 and older who finish 9th grade.
What they do
A scholarship to attend high school (diversificado) or a technical program at INTECAP, the Technical Institute for Training and Productivity, Guatemala’s public technical-training institution.
How to move ahead
After graduating from high school they can continue to college or a technical program.

12 students11 in high school and 1 at INTECAP

Stories from this stage: Franzi

See the program
Stage 6 · School path

College or technical

Who it’s for
Scholarship students who finish high school.
What they do
A scholarship for college or a technical program: the end of the school path, and often the beginning of giving back.
How to move ahead
Many come back as volunteers, facilitators or mentors for younger children.

4 students6 of 7 high school graduates went on to college in 2026

Why the Pathway exists

Middle school is where most children leave school

That’s why the Pathway doesn’t end when a child enrolls: it walks with them right through the stretch where others drop out, all the way to college.

76 %

retention in middle school: the stage where the most children in Guatemala leave school, mainly because of informal work, migration, early pregnancy and being over-age for grade.

10 de 10

scholarship students who finished 9th grade in 2026 moved on to high school. The Pathway exists so no one gets stuck at that point.

Family & community

Helping one child changes the course of the whole family

Every child comes with their family and community, and with them we build a relationship that lasts for years. In 2026 we supported 285 families, nearly twice as many as in 2025, when there were 147.

Scholarships are awarded through responsible and transparent selection processes, to support families with real needs and a strong commitment to their children’s education.

Walk with a child all the way through school

They already have the curiosity and the drive. Your support helps them keep going, alongside their family and community.
