The year in numbers

2026 in numbers

Club Extra, scholarships, Mentors and volunteering in eight communities in the department of Totonicapán.

534children enrolled in Club Extra; 332 attend on average each week, 12% more than in 2025
285families supported, 94% more than in 2025
8communities served, compared with 5 in 2025
80scholarship students: 64 in school, 11 in high school, 1 at INTECAP and 4 in college
7high school graduates, compared with 1 in 2025
232computers delivered
349children used the Chispa platform
14 of 22young people completed the 16 weeks of business mentoring
274direct beneficiaries and 41 indirect
2026 achievements

What 2026 leaves us

The results we are proudest of are the ones that show children keep moving forward, and that their families move forward with them.

  • 7 of 7 scholarship students who finished 6th grade moved on to middle school.
  • 10 of 10 scholarship students who finished 9th grade moved on to high school.
  • 6 of 7 high school graduates went on to college.
  • 3 new communities: Nimasac, Paraje Los Itzep and San Bartolo Aguas Calientes.
Looking to 2027

What's next

  • New Club Extra centers

    Bring Club Extra to more communities in Totonicapán.

  • A computer for every child

    Move toward every child having their own computer to work with.

  • First local donors

Growth year over year

Four years of growth

From 2023 to 2026, the average number of children grew from 155 to 332 and families from 98 to 285. Communities grew from 4 to 8. Not everything was growth: we had fewer school scholarship students than in 2025, and we say so just as clearly.

2030 Agenda

Our contribution to the Sustainable Development Goals

SDG 1

No poverty

We work to break the cycle of poverty through education. In 2026 we supported 285 rural families.

SDG 4

Quality education

80 scholarship students from preschool to college. All 10 who finished 9th grade moved on to high school.

SDG 8

Decent work

14 young people completed the 16 weeks of business challenges in Mentors.

SDG 10

Reduced inequalities

Totonicapán has low college enrollment. Even so, 6 of our 7 graduates went on to college.

SDG 17

Partnerships

We work with the Juan Wesley and Esmirna schools, where 64 of our scholarship students study.

Transparency

Your gift at work

Your gift supports the educational programs: scholarships, technology, mentorship and support for families. Gifts are allocated to the area of greatest need within the programs.

  • Records by community and by student, reviewed regularly.
  • Scholarships awarded through responsible and transparent selection processes.
  • No discrimination based on race, sex, color, religion or ethnic origin.
  • A 501(c)(3) registered in the United States; gifts from U.S. taxpayers are tax-deductible as allowed by law.
